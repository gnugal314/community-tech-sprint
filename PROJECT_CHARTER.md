# Project Charter

## Project identification

- **Project:** McCall Behavioral Health Network — Responsible AI for Incident Reporting
- **Program:** The Cigna Group Rapid Impact Challenge 2026
- **Volunteer team lead:** Tori Hawkins
- **Volunteer team:** Five employees total, including the team lead
- **Nonprofit working team:** One to three McCall stakeholders with relevant knowledge and decision-making authority
- **Challenge dates:** September 16–17, 2026

## Mission alignment

McCall inspires hope and promotes wellness and healing through prevention, treatment, recovery support, and community engagement for individuals and families across Connecticut. This project supports that mission by seeking safer, more timely, and more useful incident insights while preserving the human-centered nature of healthcare delivery.

## Primary question

How might McCall Behavioral Health Network leverage artificial intelligence to improve the efficiency and effectiveness of incident-report tracking, analysis, and reporting within its Quality Department while maintaining compliance, privacy, security, and appropriate human oversight?

## Current-state problem

Incident reports are submitted through email, manually entered into spreadsheets, and manually analyzed to identify patterns, trends, and quality-improvement opportunities. The process is time-intensive, may create inconsistent handoffs and data quality, and limits timely organizational insight. The exact volumes, cycle times, error rates, data elements, systems, decision points, and root causes must be validated during discovery.

## Goal statement

By 3:30 p.m. ET on September 17, the project team will provide McCall with a validated current-state assessment, prioritized improvement and AI opportunities, an agreed future-state workflow, key risk and governance considerations, and an actionable implementation roadmap. The recommendations must be traceable to McCall’s needs, feasible within its environment, understandable to intended users, and designed for responsible human oversight.

## In scope

- Current incident-report intake, entry, tracking, analysis, escalation, and reporting workflow
- Stakeholders, handoffs, manual effort, bottlenecks, pain points, controls, and root causes
- Data collection, quality, standardization, analytics, and reporting improvement opportunities
- Potential AI-assisted use cases and simpler non-AI alternatives
- Human review, exception handling, approval, auditability, and accountability
- Privacy, security, compliance, governance, acceptable-use, and risk considerations
- Organizational readiness, change management, training, and sustainability
- Prioritization based on impact, feasibility, risk, cost, and readiness
- A future-state workflow and phased implementation roadmap

## Out of scope unless explicitly approved

- Production deployment or connection to live clinical, incident, email, or NextGen systems
- Processing protected, personally identifiable, or confidential incident-level data in unapproved tools
- Legal or regulatory certification
- Replacement of McCall’s enterprise systems or selection of a vendor through procurement
- Fully autonomous incident classification, investigation, disposition, or regulatory reporting
- Clinical decision-making or recommendations about individual care
- Organization-wide AI strategy beyond lessons and reusable controls from this pilot
- Long-term implementation or operational support by the volunteer team

## Required deliverables

1. Validated need statement and project scope.
2. Current-state workflow assessment or process map.
3. Pain-point, bottleneck, and root-cause summary.
4. Prioritized opportunity matrix covering AI, automation, analytics, and process improvements.
5. Future-state workflow showing human oversight, exceptions, and controls.
6. Privacy, security, compliance, governance, and change-management considerations.
7. Phased implementation roadmap with owners, prerequisites, sequencing, and success measures.
8. Executive-ready final presentation or report, plus editable working materials as approved.

## Critical-to-quality requirements

| Requirement | Acceptance standard | Validation |
|---|---|---|
| Problem fit | Recommendations address McCall’s validated need statement | McCall stakeholder review |
| Traceability | Each recommendation maps to a pain point, root cause, or desired outcome | Deliverable review |
| Feasibility | Dependencies, cost categories, effort, constraints, and readiness are visible | Joint design review |
| Responsible AI | Human oversight, limitations, escalation, auditability, and data controls are addressed | Risk/governance review |
| Usability | Deliverables use plain language and can guide next steps | McCall walkthrough |
| Sustainability | McCall has ownership, implementation steps, and maintenance expectations | Handoff confirmation |

## Constraints

- Two virtual half-day sessions with approximately 6 hours and 40 minutes of breakout-room project time.
- Discovery and design must be time-boxed without skipping root-cause validation.
- The volunteer team provides recommendations and planning support, not legal advice or production operations.
- All information sharing must use approved systems and follow organizational policies.
- McCall is in the early stages of broader AI strategy and governance development.

## Assumptions to validate

- McCall will provide one to three knowledgeable stakeholders with decision authority.
- Relevant background materials and approved access will be available by September 15.
- Representative, sanitized examples can be used without exposing sensitive information.
- The Quality Department use case is an appropriate pilot for broader learning.
- Final deliverables can be handed over in an approved, editable format.

## Governance and scope control

- McCall owns business priorities, factual validation, implementation decisions, and final acceptance.
- Tori Hawkins facilitates decisions, manages the timeline and scope, delegates work, and escalates blockers.
- The technical/risk workstream may stop or modify a recommendation when privacy, security, compliance, safety, or feasibility concerns arise.
- New requests after scope approval must replace an item of similar effort or be placed on the follow-up roadmap.

## Success measures

- One approved need statement and scope by the end of Discover.
- All required deliverables completed or explicitly dispositioned by Day 2.
- Recommendations prioritized using agreed criteria.
- Zero sensitive records or credentials stored in GitHub or unapproved tools.
- A named McCall owner and next-step decision for each priority recommendation.
- McCall confirms that the final package is understandable, relevant, and actionable.
