# McCall Behavioral Health Network Rapid Impact Challenge

This flat, GitHub-ready project pack supports a five-person Cigna Group volunteer team partnering with one to three McCall Behavioral Health Network stakeholders during the 2026 Rapid Impact Challenge.

## Project purpose

Help McCall determine how artificial intelligence and automation could improve the efficiency and effectiveness of incident-report intake, tracking, analysis, and reporting within its Quality Department while maintaining appropriate human oversight, privacy, security, compliance, and governance.

## Organization context

McCall Behavioral Health Network is a Connecticut nonprofit with approximately 250 full-time employees, an estimated annual budget of $27 million, and more than 20 locations and community-based programs. For more than 50 years, it has delivered behavioral health, substance-use disorder treatment, integrated primary care, prevention, recovery support, community outreach, telehealth, pharmacy, and related services. Its mission is to inspire hope and promote wellness and healing through a continuum of services for individuals and families of all ages.

Website: https://mccallbhn.org

## Challenge context

Incident reports are currently submitted by email, manually entered into spreadsheets, and manually analyzed for patterns, trends, and quality-improvement opportunities. The process is time-intensive and limits timely insight. McCall is early in its broader AI strategy, is planning an internal AI workgroup, and has begun exploring governance, acceptable-use, privacy, and risk-management practices. This Quality Department use case may serve as a responsible pilot for later adoption elsewhere.

## Intended outcomes

By the end of Day 2, the teams should have:

1. A validated need statement and current-state workflow assessment.
2. Documented manual steps, bottlenecks, risks, and root causes.
3. A prioritized set of AI-enabled and non-AI improvement opportunities.
4. An agreed future-state workflow with human review and escalation points.
5. Compliance, privacy, security, governance, and change-management considerations.
6. A practical implementation roadmap based on impact, feasibility, risk, cost, and organizational readiness.
7. Final deliverables in a format McCall can understand, own, and use.

The event does not require a production-ready AI application. A prototype should be attempted only if the teams approve it as feasible, safe, useful, and secondary to the required recommendations and implementation plan.

## Working principles

- Listen and validate before proposing solutions.
- Improve the process before automating it.
- Use sanitized or approved information; never place sensitive client or incident data in GitHub.
- Keep the scope achievable within two half-day sessions.
- Prioritize maintainability, organizational readiness, and human-centered care.
- Use plain language and clearly distinguish assumptions from verified facts.
- Obtain McCall approval before moving from discovery to design and from design to development.
- Store working materials in the approved shared folder; use GitHub only for approved, non-sensitive project artifacts.
- Ensure McCall leaves with ownership, clear next steps, and an implementable plan.

## Repository rules

- Keep project artifacts in this single flat directory.
- Do not commit protected health information, personally identifiable information, incident narratives, credentials, access tokens, internal-only screenshots, or confidential source documents.
- Use role-based placeholders in public or broadly shared artifacts.
- Store source materials in the approved secure shared folder and link to them only when permitted.
- Record scope, recommendation, risk, and acceptance decisions in `DECISION_LOG.csv`.
- Treat McCall as the decision owner for business priorities and final acceptance.
