# McCall Discovery Guide

## Discovery objective

Become sufficiently familiar with McCall’s incident-reporting challenge to define the actual need, identify root causes, establish success criteria, and create a realistic scope before recommending technology.

## Facilitator reminders

- Ask open-ended questions and actively listen.
- Treat McCall representatives as subject-matter experts in their mission, operations, people, and constraints.
- Resist proposing a solution while information is still being gathered.
- Separate verified facts, stakeholder perceptions, assumptions, and unanswered questions.
- Confirm understanding frequently: “What we heard is… Did we get that right?”
- Do not request or display identifiable incident information when sanitized examples are sufficient.

## Need statement

Complete and obtain McCall confirmation:

`The Quality Department needs a better way to [perform the job] because [validated root causes], which currently results in [measurable operational or quality impact]. Success would mean [observable result] while maintaining [required human, privacy, security, and compliance controls].`

## Discovery question bank

### Mission, users, and value

- Why is this challenge important to McCall and the people it serves?
- How does it connect to current strategy, care quality, workforce efficiency, and staff experience?
- Who submits, receives, reviews, investigates, analyzes, approves, and acts on incident reports?
- Which users experience the greatest burden or risk?
- What would success look like at the end of the challenge and after 30, 90, and 180 days?

### Current workflow

- Please walk us through one representative incident from submission through final reporting and follow-up.
- What triggers the process, and what marks completion?
- Which email inboxes, spreadsheets, systems, templates, or reports are involved?
- Is NextGen or another platform involved at any point? If so, how?
- Where is information re-entered, reformatted, reconciled, or manually categorized?
- What approvals, investigations, escalations, or notifications occur?
- Which steps are working well and should be preserved?

### Volume, effort, and performance

- Approximately how many reports are received by day, week, or month?
- How much staff time is spent on intake, entry, cleanup, analysis, and reporting?
- What are the common delays, errors, duplicates, missing fields, or rework?
- How timely must insights or escalations be?
- What measures are currently reported, to whom, and at what frequency?
- What baseline evidence is available without exposing sensitive data?

### Data and analytics

- What fields are collected, which are structured, and which are free text?
- Are categories and definitions standardized across programs and locations?
- What patterns or trends are most important to identify?
- How are data quality and completeness validated today?
- What reports or visualizations are most valuable, and what decisions do they support?
- Which outputs must remain explainable, auditable, and reproducible?

### Controls, risk, and human oversight

- What information is considered sensitive or restricted?
- Which internal policies and applicable privacy, security, record-retention, and reporting requirements govern the workflow?
- What decisions must always remain with qualified staff?
- Where should AI suggestions require review, approval, correction, or escalation?
- What error would create the greatest harm?
- What audit trail, access control, monitoring, and incident-response capability would be required?
- Which tools are currently approved, and what restrictions apply to generative AI?

### Readiness and sustainability

- What AI or automation experiments have already occurred, including Microsoft Copilot?
- What did McCall learn from those experiments?
- What is the planned role of the internal AI workgroup?
- Who would own this pilot, its data, its controls, and its ongoing performance?
- What budget, procurement, integration, training, or capacity constraints matter most?
- How much technical support can McCall realistically sustain?
- Who must support or approve implementation after the challenge?

### Deliverable preferences

- Which format will be most useful: presentation, written report, workflow diagram, matrix, implementation roadmap, or editable combination?
- Who is the primary audience for the final package?
- What level of technical detail is appropriate?
- What must be “bought in” for the recommendations to move forward?

## Current-state assessment

Capture the following for every major step:

| Element | Questions to answer |
|---|---|
| Trigger and input | What starts the step and what information is required? |
| Actor and system | Who performs it and where? |
| Action and decision | What happens and what judgment is applied? |
| Output and recipient | What is produced and who receives it? |
| Time and volume | How long and how often? |
| Pain point | What delay, error, rework, burden, or risk occurs? |
| Control | How is accuracy, privacy, security, or compliance protected? |
| Root cause | Why does the pain point occur? |

## Opportunity categories

Consider improvements in this order:

1. Eliminate unnecessary steps and clarify ownership.
2. Standardize forms, fields, categories, definitions, and validation.
3. Automate deterministic routing, reminders, transfers, and calculations.
4. Improve governed analytics, dashboards, and recurring reporting.
5. Use AI assistance only where it adds value, such as summarization, suggested classification, trend discovery, or draft narratives, with human validation.

## Opportunity scoring

Rate each proposed approach from 1 (poor) to 5 (strong), then multiply by the weight.

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Mission, quality, and user impact | 25 |
| Feasibility within McCall’s environment | 20 |
| Privacy, security, compliance, and safety fit | 20 |
| Organizational readiness and maintainability | 15 |
| Time or effort reduction | 10 |
| Explainability and human oversight | 10 |

Any fatal privacy, security, compliance, safety, or data-access constraint disqualifies an option regardless of total score.

## Discovery exit criteria

- Need statement confirmed by McCall.
- Current workflow and key stakeholders understood.
- Root causes distinguished from symptoms.
- Success criteria and measures agreed.
- In-scope and out-of-scope boundaries documented.
- Required materials, access, decisions, and owners identified.
- Unresolved questions captured with owners and deadlines.
