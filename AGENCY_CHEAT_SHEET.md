# McCall Agency Cheat Sheet and Discovery Talking Points

Purpose: Help The Cigna Group volunteer team understand McCall Behavioral Health Network, the communities it serves, and how the incident-reporting challenge connects to McCall's mission, care model, and quality outcomes.

## Executive TLDR

McCall Behavioral Health Network is a nonprofit behavioral health provider serving individuals and families across western Connecticut through prevention, treatment, recovery support, primary care, outreach, and community engagement. McCall describes its mission as inspiring hope and promoting wellness and healing through a continuum of health services for people of all ages and families across Connecticut. Source: [Who We Are](https://mccallbhn.org/about/who-we-are/)

For this challenge, the Quality Department's incident-reporting workflow should be treated as a mission-critical quality improvement process, not just an administrative workflow. Better intake, tracking, analysis, and reporting can help McCall identify patterns faster, reduce manual burden, strengthen learning across programs, and protect the human-centered nature of care.

The volunteer team's best posture is: listen first, map the current state, protect sensitive information, distinguish process fixes from AI opportunities, and leave McCall with a practical pilot roadmap they can own.

## Challenge Lens: What Problem Are We Really Solving?

McCall's stated challenge has two connected parts:

1. The process problem: Incident reports are submitted by email, manually entered into spreadsheets, and manually analyzed. This creates administrative burden, delays insight, and may make it harder to identify trends and quality-improvement opportunities.
2. The AI opportunity: McCall is exploring how AI and automation might improve workforce efficiency, decision-making, reporting, and staff satisfaction while preserving privacy, security, compliance, and human-centered care.

Talking point: "Before we recommend a tool, we want to understand the current workflow, what decisions the data supports, where the manual burden sits, and which safeguards McCall needs in place."

## Discovery Call Cues

Use these prompts to keep the discussion grounded and executive-friendly:

- "What would a better incident-reporting process allow the Quality Department to do sooner or better?"
- "Which steps are manual because they are simply repetitive, and which steps are manual because they require judgment?"
- "What are the most important reporting outputs today: compliance reporting, leadership trends, quality improvement, site/program comparison, follow-up tracking, or something else?"
- "What does McCall need to preserve from the current process?"
- "What data elements are essential, and which should never be used outside approved systems?"
- "What systems are involved today: email, spreadsheets, EHR, SharePoint, Teams, Power BI, ticketing, or other tools?"
- "What would be a useful sprint deliverable: workflow redesign, opportunity matrix, roadmap, prototype, governance checklist, reporting mockup, or a combination?"
- "Who would own and maintain the solution after the challenge?"

## Recommendations for Volunteer Support

### 1. Start with a current-state workflow and data map

The volunteers should map how an incident moves from submission to triage, spreadsheet entry, review, analysis, reporting, action, and closure. Include who touches the process, what systems are used, what fields are captured, where rework occurs, and which controls exist.

Best-fit roles:

- Process and Workflow Lead: Facilitate the workflow map and pain-point inventory.
- Data and Analytics Lead: Identify required fields, reporting outputs, data quality issues, and metric definitions.
- Security and Change Lead: Flag sensitive data, access points, retention concerns, and role-based controls.

### 2. Separate "automation candidates" from "AI candidates"

Many improvements may not require generative AI. The team should classify opportunities into process standardization, forms/intake, workflow automation, reporting/BI, and AI-assisted analysis. This keeps the recommendation practical and reduces risk.

Best-fit roles:

- AI and Automation Lead: Define where Copilot, Power Automate, or approved AI tools may responsibly assist.
- Technical Lead/Architect: Assess fit with McCall's Microsoft 365, EHR, email, spreadsheet, and infrastructure environment.
- Security and Change Lead: Identify guardrails for PHI, access, auditability, human approval, and acceptable use.

### 3. Leave McCall with a phased pilot roadmap

The final deliverable should not be "use AI." It should give McCall a sequenced pilot path: stabilize intake, standardize fields, automate low-risk routing/status steps, improve reporting, then evaluate AI-assisted classification/summarization only after governance and data controls are clear.

Best-fit roles:

- Team Lead: Keep scope tight, confirm decisions, and integrate final deliverables.
- AI and Automation Lead: Build the opportunity matrix and define AI/non-AI options.
- Data and Analytics Lead: Recommend reporting measures, dashboard concepts, and validation approach.
- Process and Workflow Lead: Design future-state workflow with human review points.
- Security and Change Lead: Add privacy, compliance, training, adoption, and risk controls.
- Infrastructure/Technical Architecture support: Clarify implementation constraints, system dependencies, data movement, licensing, hosting, and maintainability.

## Team Lead Guidance: Rally the Team Around the Mission

Lead with the mission before the method. McCall's work is about hope, wellness, healing, connection, and whole-person care. The incident-reporting challenge matters because better quality data can help the organization see patterns, respond faster, reduce staff burden, and protect the people it serves.

Recommended opening frame for the volunteer team:

"Our job is not to force an AI solution into McCall's workflow. Our job is to understand how incident reporting affects quality, staff capacity, and client safety, then give McCall practical recommendations they can trust, govern, and maintain. If we do that well, we help free up more of their time for the people and communities they serve."

Set the team up for success:

- Assign each volunteer a listening lane before the connector call: process, data/reporting, AI/automation, security/risk, and technical feasibility.
- Ask everyone to bring two discovery questions and one assumption they want to validate.
- Use a parking lot for attractive ideas that are out of scope or too risky for the sprint.
- Require every recommendation to map to a validated pain point, McCall outcome, owner, dependency, and risk consideration.
- Protect trust: do not request sensitive incident narratives, client identifiers, screenshots with PHI, credentials, or live-system access unless McCall and program leadership explicitly approve it.
- Keep the sprint human-centered: AI should support staff judgment, not replace clinical, compliance, or quality ownership.

## Best Discovery Posture

Be curious, humble, and structured. McCall knows its clients, staff, programs, constraints, and compliance responsibilities. The volunteer team brings outside perspective, process discipline, technical thinking, and rapid synthesis. The strongest outcome is a recommendation package McCall recognizes as realistic, respectful, and ready for next-step decision-making.
